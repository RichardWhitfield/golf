# Content & Coaching Model

What the site actually says, and the structure underneath it. This is the domain — the golf
knowledge the app is built around. Keep this accurate; the UI serves it, not the other way round.

---

## The premise

**The fault is sequencing, not the hands.** The player slices because the upper body starts the
downswing, throwing the club over the top and producing an out-to-in path. It is *already
proven* fixable: when the player steps into the shot, the slice disappears. Everything in the
plan recreates that step feeling while standing still.

**The one idea:** *Lower body leads, and the club drops into the slot.*

## The KPI

One number, measured on a Trackman: **club path, with the driver**.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Current | `−6°` to `−10°` (out-to-in — the slice) |
| Target | `−2°` to `+2°` (roughly neutral) |

The goal is **not** a big draw. The path needs to return toward neutral with the face just left
of it. **Judge the trend across a session, never a single shot.**

### Why the driver, and why the club is named

The KPI used to be "club path" with no club attached. Against thirteen months of real data that
is not measurable — a blended average tracks which club was in your hand as much as what the
swing did.

Read November 2025. Blended, it was the best month in the whole series at `−3.27°`. The driver
that month was `−7.79°` — the *worst* to that point. The number only looked good because
42 seven-irons at `−1.66°` outweighed 22 drivers. Over the same thirteen months the driver went
backwards (`−4.01°` → `−7.50°`) while the 4-iron improved (`−7.19°` → `−4.73°`). One blended
series cannot show both. It shows neither.

The driver is the KPI because it is where the slice costs most, it has the most measured shots
after the 4-iron, and it is the club currently trending the wrong way.

### The band is shared, but the clubs are not

`−2°` to `+2°` is the target for every club, not a per-club figure. It is a coaching target, and
inventing a separate band per club would turn "where you have been" into "where you should be".

Be honest about what that means when reading the numbers: **the driver sits systematically
shallower than the irons** throughout the data, which is expected from a flatter plane and an
upward attack angle. An iron inside the band is a stronger result than a driver inside it.

### Every average needs its count

**Never read a club-path average without its shot count.** July's 7-iron figure of `−10.27°` is
ten shots. The tail of every series is thin, and a mean over a handful of swings will be
over-read every time unless the `n` is sitting next to it. A hand-typed session has no count,
and shows a dash rather than a number invented to fill the gap.

**And read each metric's own count.** Every metric carries its own `n`, because the bay does not
measure them equally. Over the same driver strokes there are 723 carry readings, 666 for swing
plane, 618 for club path and 556 for face to path. Two numbers side by side on one card can be
backed by very different amounts of evidence.

### The face is not the fault

Across thirteen months the driver's face has sat within a degree of square — the middle reading is
`−0.86°`. Face to path is the number that has been open: a median of `+4.8°`, and **never once
negative**. The closest it has come across 44 driver sessions is `+0.97°`, and the curve has never
dropped below `+3.61 m`. There is no session on record where the driver did not slice on average.

That is not a face problem. The face is square; the path is so far left that square *is* open
relative to it. **Fix the path and the curve goes with it.** The face needs nothing.

This is the KPI confirmed by the data rather than by argument, and it is why `/progress` leads
its driver section with path and face to path side by side.

### Swing plane was the wrong suspect

A steeper plane was the obvious explanation for an out-to-in path. It was worth testing, and it
does not hold up.

On the driver, across 44 sessions, a steeper plane has gone with a **less** out-to-in path —
`r = +0.503`, moderate, and signed against the hypothesis. At the 4-iron, measured shot by shot,
the relationship disappears entirely (`−0.053`). Steepness is not what is sending the club left.

Keep the number. Stop treating it as the cause. The site recomputes that correlation from your
own sessions every time the page loads, so if the swing changes the answer changes with it.

## Drills

Seven drills in three groups. Each is tagged for where it can be done:

- **`SIM`** — requires the Monday simulator/Trackman bay.
- **`HOME`** — can be done outdoors at home with airflow balls.

Drills carry a stable number (`01`–`07`) that the weekly schedule references by digit. **These
numbers are identifiers — never renumber them**, or every cross-reference on the site breaks.

Each drill also carries `defaultSwings` — a whole number the log form pre-fills. Separate from
`reps`, which stays prose because ranges and mixed counts ("10 rehearsals + 5 hits") carry intent
that a single number would lose. Change both together when a drill's volume changes.

### Group 1 — Sequencing & transition

**01 · Step-change** — `SIM` `HOME` — 10–15 reps
Feet together, ball forward. Start back, and as you reach the top, step the lead foot toward the
target and swing.
*Feels like:* pressure in the lead foot before the arms move.
> The money drill, and the designated **on-course reset** whenever the slice creeps back.

**02 · Pump-and-go** — `SIM` `HOME` — 8–10 reps
Swing to the top. Pump the hands down toward the trail pocket 2–3 times by bumping and rotating
the hips, arms passive, then release the last one and strike.
*Feels like:* hands dropping while the hips clear.

**03 · Pause-at-the-top** — `SIM` `HOME` — 10 reps
Full backswing, freeze for a genuine one-count, then start down with the lead hip.
*Feels like:* an unhurried change of direction.
> Kills the rushed upper-body snatch.

### Group 2 — Slot & path

**04 · Outside gate** — `SIM` `HOME` — 15–20 balls
Headcover or spare airflow ball a grip-length outside the ball and slightly behind it, on the
line. Over-the-top clips it; an inside path misses clean.
*Feels like:* club coming from behind you, not across.

**05 · Trail-arm only** — `HOME` — 15–20 reps
Trail hand only, choked down, three-quarter swings at airflow balls.
*Feels like:* the forearm rotating over through impact.
> You physically cannot come over the top one-handed.

**06 · Angled-stick shallow** — `SIM` — 10 rehearsals + 5 hits
Alignment stick in the ground angled up and away, just outside the trail hip. Swing so the club
stays under it coming down.
*Feels like:* the club travelling below the shaft line.

### Group 3 — Tempo (the quiet fix)

**07 · Slow-motion & swishes** — `HOME` — 20–30 reps / 2–3 min
Half-speed full swings, or swishes with a weighted trainer, focused only on the order:
**pressure → hips → torso → arms → club**.
*Feels like:* smooth, in no hurry, the club whipping late.

## The week

### Monday — 2 hours on Trackman

| Time | Block | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 10 min | Warm-up | Easy wedges and tempo swishes. No swing thoughts. |
| 40 min | Block + data | Drills 04 & 06, then 02. Hit, check the path number, repeat. |
| 40 min | Transfer | Alternate one drill-swing, one normal swing holding the same path. |
| 25 min | Play holes | Different clubs and targets, full routine each ball. Score how many stay left of a slice. |
| 5 min | Log it | Note best and typical path for the session. |

The final 5-minute "log it" block is the hook the app hangs off — the habit already exists in
the plan.

### Tuesday–Sunday — 5–10 min, outdoors, airflow balls

| Day | Drills |
|---|---|
| Tue | 05 + 07 |
| Wed | 01 + 04 |
| Thu | 02 + 07 |
| Fri | 05 + 04 |
| Sat | Whatever felt worst + a few steps to reset |
| Sun | Free choice, finish on a good one |

> **Two drills a session. Slow and correct beats fast and many.**

Saturday and Sunday are deliberately unprescribed — the app must allow free-choice logging, not
force a schedule.

## The three-week arc

**Week 1 · Groove the feel** — Block-heavy. Drills 01, 02, 05. Make the lower-body-led move feel
normal and pull the path into the −2° to −4° range. Don't worry yet how it holds up on random
shots.

**Week 2 · Transfer** — Mix drill-swings with normal swings. Trust the path number without the
exaggeration. Add a simple trigger: one look at the target and the thought *"lower body first."*

**Week 3 · Proof it** — Random practice only. Never the same club twice, always a full routine.
The swing must hold when you're not thinking mechanics. Step drill remains the on-course reset.

This is a *block → transfer → random* progression, standard motor-learning structure. It matters
for the app: **the same drill means something different in week 1 than in week 3**, so progress
should be read within a phase, not flatly across all three weeks.

## Training aids

1. **Alignment sticks** — Cheap, essential, power half the drills (gates, angled shallowing
   stick, path and alignment checks). Buy first.
2. **Orange Whip Trainer** — Weighted flexible shaft, usable outdoors in a small space. Punishes
   an arms-first move.
3. **Lag Shot 7-iron** — Whippy training club that hits airflow balls. The one aid giving real
   feedback outdoors between Monday sessions.

*Optional, later:* Tour Striker PlaneMate or GravityFit — bigger investment, not needed for these
three weeks.

## Watch-outs

- **Don't overcook it.** Pulls or hooks mean the path has gone too far in-to-out, or the face is
  shutting. Ease off the draw exaggeration and let the number settle near neutral.
- **Fatigue equals slice.** Tiredness causes rushing and reverting to arms. If a session is going
  sideways, do three step drills and finish on a good feel.
- **Trust the number, not the day.** Ball flight varies; the path *trend* over a session is the
  honest measure.

These have direct app implications — see `architecture.md`. In particular, a path that overshoots
past `+2°` is a *problem*, not a success, so any progress visual needs a target **band**, not a
"higher is better" bar.

## Content principles

1. **Voice is second-person, direct, and coach-like.** Short declaratives. "Your money drill."
   "Kills the rushed upper-body snatch." Not academic, not chatty.
2. **Every drill has a *feel* cue, not just a mechanic.** This is the most valuable field —
   preserve it prominently in any redesign.
3. **British English** (`lang="en-GB"`): metre, colour, practise (verb) / practice (noun).
4. **Numbers are specific.** Rep ranges, degrees, minutes. Never "a few" where a range exists.
5. **The plan is opinionated.** It prescribes rather than offers options. Interactivity should
   make it easier to *follow*, not turn it into a menu of choices.

## Content beyond three weeks

The plan is explicitly 3 weeks, ending before a trip. Once complete, the app needs an answer to
"what now?" — likely one of: repeat with a raised target, switch KPI (face angle, strike
location), or archive the block and start a new one. **Undecided** — see `roadmap.md`.
