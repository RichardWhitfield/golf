This is your one KPI on the Trackman. You don't need a big draw — you need the path to come back toward neutral with the face just left of it. Watch the number trend across a session, not any single shot.

01

The one idea behind everything

Lower body leads, and the club drops into the slot. The step drill already does this for you — it shifts pressure to your lead foot and gives the hips a head start, so the club falls to the inside instead of being thrown over the top. Every drill below recreates that step feeling standing still.

Step-change

Your money drill. Feet together, ball forward. Start back, and as you reach the top, step the lead foot toward the target and swing. Your on-course reset whenever the slice creeps back.

Reps · 10–15Feels like · pressure in the lead foot before the arms move

SIMHOME

Pump-and-go

Swing to the top. Pump the hands down toward the trail pocket 2–3 times by bumping and rotating the hips — arms passive — then let the last one go and strike.

Reps · 8–10Feels like · hands dropping while the hips clear

SIMHOME

Pause-at-the-top

Full backswing, freeze for a genuine one-count, then start down with the lead hip. Kills the rushed upper-body snatch that throws the club out.

Reps · 10Feels like · an unhurried change of direction

Outside gate

Set a headcover (or spare airflow ball) a grip-length outside the ball and slightly behind it, on the line. Over-the-top clips it; an inside path misses it clean.

Reps · 15–20 ballsFeels like · club coming from behind you, not across

Trail-arm only

Trail hand only, choked down, small three-quarter swings at airflow balls. You physically can't come over the top one-handed — the arm has to drop and rotate.

Reps · 15–20Feels like · the forearm rotating over through impact

Angled-stick shallow

Push an alignment stick into the ground angled up and away, just outside the trail hip. Swing so the club stays under the stick coming down. A clear visual for the slot.

Reps · 10 rehearsals + 5 hitsFeels like · the club travelling below the shaft line

Slow-motion & swishes

Half-speed full swings, or swishes with a weighted/flexible trainer, focused only on the order: pressure → hips → torso → arms → club. Rushing from the top is what fires the arms first.

Reps · 20–30 / 2–3 minFeels like · smooth, in no hurry, the club whipping late

04

The 3-week arc

01
Week one

Groove the feel

Block-heavy. Lots of Drills 1, 2, 5. Make the lower-body-led move feel normal and pull the path into the −2° to −4° range. Don't worry yet how it holds up on random shots.

02
Week two

Transfer

Mix drill-swings with normal swings. Trust the path number without the exaggeration. Add a simple trigger — one look at the target and the thought "lower body first."

03
Week three

Proof it

Random practice only — never the same club twice, always a full routine. The swing needs to hold when you're not thinking mechanics. Step drill stays as your on-course reset.

05

Training aids

1

Alignment sticks

Cheap, essential, and they power half the drills here — gates, the angled shallowing stick, path and alignment checks. Buy these first.

2

Orange Whip Trainer

Weighted, flexible shaft you can swing outdoors in a small space. The floppy shaft punishes an arms-first move and rewards proper sequencing.

3

Lag Shot 7-iron

A whippy training club you can actually hit airflow balls with — the one aid that gives you real feedback outdoors between Monday sessions. Come over the top and you feel it instantly.

Optional, later: a swing-plane trainer like the Tour Striker PlaneMate or a GravityFit setup — a bigger investment, and not needed for these three weeks.

Watch-outs
  • Don't overcook it. Pulling or hooking means the path has gone too far in-to-out, or the face is shutting. Ease off the "draw" exaggeration and let the number settle near neutral.
  • Fatigue equals slice. Tiredness makes you rush and revert to arms — that's why Monday was worse. If a session's going sideways, do three step drills and finish on a good feel.
  • Trust the number, not the day. Ball flight varies. The path trend over a session is the honest measure of progress.
